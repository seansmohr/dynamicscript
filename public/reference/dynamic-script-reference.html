Standing compliance rule

Before anything else

A client who is 65 or older in California cannot be pitched ancillary — just pitch Medicare. Cancer / heart attack / stroke cover and skilled nursing cover cannot be sold, so do not embed either exposure — skip the major-exposures set-up, the CHS conversation and the skilled nursing conversation entirely.

Dental can still be sold. Ask whether it is important to them as normal; it still prices $50 into the Medicare Supplement umbrella. That leaves the Advantage umbrella at $0, since an Advantage plan already bundles dental.

Gold / Silver / Bronze is cancer, heart attack and stroke cover, so it cannot be sold either. On the employer track there is no Medicare plan to offer in its place, so that call ends after the delay recommendation and the CMS L564 education.

Their zip code and date of birth decide all of this, and both are the first two questions of the needs assessment.

3 · Education (Employer)

Employer coverage with over 20 employees, after the full needs assessment. This track ends here: no Supplement, no Advantage, no umbrella.

Recommendation #1 — delay Medicare

"So right now this transition to Medicare does not make much sense for you, so my first recommendation would be to delay Part A and Part B entirely. You have a strong plan with your employer and your premiums are pretty low."

Peace of mind on penalties

"And I want to make sure you are at peace of mind with penalties, since that's always a big concern. You won't be penalized for delaying Medicare since you have creditable coverage with your employer. Are we clear on that?"
Wait for the response. Do not move on without a yes.

CMS L564 — the hall pass

"Now when you do decide to retire, or you lose that creditable coverage, that's when you take action. That's when you take CMS L564 — it's a form you'll take to your HR department and they'll help you fill it out. Then you'll take that form and you're gonna submit that form along with your application for Part A and Part B. That form is basically your hall pass that voids all penalties. Are we clear on that?"
California, 65 or older — stop here

Gold / Silver / Bronze is cancer, heart attack and stroke cover, so it cannot be sold, and there is no Medicare plan to offer in its place because they are staying on employer coverage. End the call as education. Answer their questions, make sure they know to call when they lose creditable coverage, and note when to follow up.

6 · Medicare Supplement education

"So for Medicare Supplement, you are going to have an additional premium on top of your Part B premium, but it is the more comprehensive option. You don't have networks, you don't need prior authorizations, you don't need referrals — it gives you the flexibility and freedom to go where you want."
"You'll have an annual deductible of $283, and once you hit that deductible all other Medicare-approved expenses are covered at 100%. For all non-Medicare approved expenses, that's what the umbrella coverage is for, and we'll get to that in more detail very soon."
California and 65 or older: leave out the umbrella sentence — the umbrella can't cover non-approved expenses there.
"And if dental is important to you, that will be a separate premium, and you'll need a separate prescription drug plan as well."
"So to recap Medicare Supplement: you'll be responsible for an additional premium, you'll have the freedom to go where you want when you want, and your Medicare-approved expenses will be very predictable."
"Now do you have any questions on how a Medicare Supplement functions?"

7 · Medicare Advantage (Part C) education

"Now for Medicare Advantage, it's going to be more like your all in one plan, so not only is it going to include your health insurance, but it will also include dental, vision, hearing and prescription drug coverage."
"Now you can have lower monthly premiums with an advantage plan, but you will have copays for doctor visits and coinsurance for major procedures, so that's why we never recommend it without umbrella coverage."
California and 65 or older: drop "so that's why we never recommend it without umbrella coverage" — there is no Advantage umbrella to sell there.
"And if this is the route you take, you have to be comfortable with prior authorizations, being in a network, and needing referrals to see specialists. We like to think 9 times out of 10 Medicare Advantage is a better version of your current plan, because these plans usually have little to no deductible and very affordable copays."
"Any questions on how a Medicare Advantage plan functions?"
